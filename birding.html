Birding

My lifelong hobby

I have been a birder since before I can remember. My mom's parents were both huge birders, and they got me into it at a very young age. There is a picture of me, still in diapers, lying on the floor of their house "reading" the National Geographic Field Guide to the Birds of North America (upside down). Ever since then, I have been keeping track of the birds I've seen, whether I'm sitting by the window at home or hiking on some far-off continent. I have had some absolutely unbelievable experiences while birding; some of my favorites have been:

  • Watching massive royal albatrosses soar aft of our research vessel while working in the Humboldt Current.
  • Sitting with Kenn Kaufman watching a northern parula build a nest on Hog Island in Maine.
  • Banding shorebirds with International Birding and Research Center Eilat on the shores of the Red Sea.
  • Playing hooky from work for an afternoon to chase (and see) Massachusetts's first ever american flamingo.
  • Hanging on to the outside of a massive combine harvesting rice watching rails and bitterns take flight.
  • Spotting, photographing, and later publishing the first recorded melanistic bird identified on Puerto Rico.
  • Being startled by a mountain tapir while scoping a mixed flocks in the cloud forests of Ecuador.
  • Banding northern saw-whet owls in Hopkins Memorial Forest while at Williams College.
  • Seeing a diademed sandpiper-plover confidently climb on my friend's back in the high Andes.
  • Giving a mixed research and birding talk about my fieldwork offshore Chile to the Brookline Bird Club.
